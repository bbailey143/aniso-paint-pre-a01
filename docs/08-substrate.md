# CARD 8 — Substrate (Paper & Canvas)

The paper is not a backdrop — its tooth and absorbency shape every mark. A rapid
stroke on rough paper breaks up; a slow deliberate one lays smooth and connected.
That behaviour is physics here, not a texture overlay. Each surface is a data row.

## Parameter set (from GUIDE + C97/Y13, structured)

| Parameter | Range | Physical meaning | Drives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tooth** | hot press / cold press / rough | surface peak-to-valley amplitude | height-field amplitude; drybrush skip; where heavy pigment settles |
| **Sizing** | gelatin sized ↔ unsized | cellulose barrier slowing absorption | absorption rate; whether pigment sits up brilliant or sinks dull |
| **Weight** | 90 / 140 / 300 lb | fibre mass, water capacity | how long the sheet stays workable; buckling |
| **Fibre** | 100 % cotton rag ↔ wood pulp | fibre length/strength | whether scrubbing and lifting work or tear |
| **Capillary radius `r_c`** | 0 (canvas) → 2.5e-4 (absorbent paper) | pore size | absorption speed via Lucas-Washburn |

Four to five numbers per surface. Every paper and canvas is a data row, exactly like
a brush or a medium.

## Lucas-Washburn absorption [Y13 §4.3]

Penetration of liquid into porous material:
```
dl/dt = (P_h + P_c) / (8μl) × (r_c² + 4r_c)
P_c   = (2γ / r_c) · cos θ
```
`l` = length penetrated, `r_c` = capillary radius, `P_h` = hydrostatic pressure,
`P_c` = capillary pressure, `μ` = viscosity, `γ` = surface tension, `θ` = contact angle.

`[KEY]` **`r_c` is the single absorptiveness dial.** Y13: `r_c = 0` for canvas (no
absorption — oil/acrylic), `2.5e-5`–`2.5e-4` for watercolour paper. That is hot press
vs rough, and paper vs canvas, as one physical parameter. The equation gives velocity
explicitly, so it integrates cleanly onto the grid.

## Size exclusion — why granulation happens [Y13 §3]

Three things reach the surface and separate **by particle size**:
- **Solvent** — smallest, penetrates deepest and fastest.
- **Binder** — less absorbable; some remains on the surface and gives the rough texture.
- **Pigment** — grains 0.001–0.002 mm, **larger than the pores**, so pigment stays on
  the surface and settles into the valleys.

That is a physical explanation of granulation, better than any heuristic. On rough
paper, heavy mineral pigments settle into the low points (Stokes' Law); the render's
relief lighting then makes them read as grain.

## Paper structure [C97 §2.1]

Modelled as a **height field `h` + fluid capacity field `c`**, with
`c = h(c_max − c_min) + c_min` and `h` generated by pseudo-random processes scaled to
`0 < h < 1`. The height field's **slope** modifies fluid velocity (streaks parallel
to flow) and, via A26's local-gravity trick, is where paint pools.

## The build rows

`[BUILD]` **HotPress** (low tooth, small `r_c` → hard-edge washes), **ColdPress**
(moderate tooth), **Rough** (high tooth, large `r_c` → drybrush skip and granulation).
`[future]` **Canvas** (`r_c = 0`) when oil/acrylic land.

## Parked (from GUIDE)

`[UNVERIFIED]` Lightweight (90 lb) paper buckles on contact with water, creating
troughs where wash pools — A26's local-gravity trick with a physical cause (the
paper's own deformation becoming the height field gravity reads). Every watercolourist
knows this; no simulator appears to model it. FEM (`K{u} = {F_moisture}`) is the
GUIDE's route. Canvas weave/slubs and oil-vs-acrylic ground delamination are also
parked here.
